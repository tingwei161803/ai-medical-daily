Friday, November 20, 2026 · Covering Nov 10–19 · Taipei time

Friday's brief is to take one or two products apart, and this week's material set the selection criteria for us: between November 10 and 19, at least seven companies shipped different versions of the same thing — an agent layer stacked on the electronic record, handling prior authorization, documentation gaps, the inbox and discharge barriers. What is worth dismantling is not their feature lists but where each one has placed its moat, and whether it will let anyone else do the measuring. Two today: Google Cloud's Gemini for Healthcare (11/10, in preview, seven launch customers, read-only on Epic and Oracle Health with no write-back, zero performance figures and zero pricing), and Suki's Suki OS (11/16, a prior authorization agent and a CDI agent, 450+ organizations, carrying a up-to-100% money-back guarantee whose terms are unpublished). The first bets on breadth, the second on risk-bearing. The counterweight comes last: the week's only regulator-checked numbers belong to a single narrow feature — where is the feeding tube — in GE HealthCare's FDA 510(k).

01 — Top Stories

Eight items ordered by distance from the chart: from the outer ring that cannot reach it, to the only layer that can write, to the party still explaining its own overrun
Platform Google CloudGemini for Healthcare11/10

Google Cloud's Gemini for Healthcare can read Epic — and is explicitly barred from writing to it

What

Google Cloud launched Gemini for Healthcare on November 10, in preview, for Gemini Enterprise Standard and Plus customers, with a choice of per-user seat subscriptions or pay-as-you-go pricing; the announcement lists no prices. The product is five things: five preset skills (records synthesis, pre-appointment prep, coverage and policy review, payer benefit determination among them); EHR connectors (Epic via open.epic APIs, Oracle Health via Ignite FHIR APIs, read access only, with no write-back to the EHR); administrative and standards lookups (CMS coverage data, the NPI Registry, RxNorm, ICD-10, IMO Health's Knowledge Graph); medical repositories (Optum EvidenceVue, ASCO guidelines, BMJ Digital Health); and model choice — Google, Anthropic and OpenAI models side by side, with smart routing sending high-volume administrative work to cheaper models and complex analysis to high-reasoning ones. Launch customers are Northwell Health, HCA Healthcare, Optum, Highmark Health, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Massachusetts, Hartford HealthCare and Stanford School of Medicine, with ASCO contributing content. The demo Fortune watched took 35 seconds to pull a patient's conditions, medications, allergies and labs, compare them against an outside specialist's report, and flag new lab results and a missed allergy.

Why it matters

The no-write-back line is the shape of the whole strategy. Google is not selling a system of record; it is selling a read-and-orchestrate layer above one, so its only possible moat is the breadth of its connectors — which is exactly what the partner announcements of November 17–19 were filling in: Keragon wiring 400+ healthcare applications through a single MCP connection, and Verato turning patient identity resolution into an MCP server. The demand-side numbers are all in the release: clinicians lose nearly 28 hours a week to documentation and administrative work (a Harris Poll/Google Cloud study), 41.9% of physicians reported burnout (2025 AMA report), and administrative costs run 25% to 35% of US health spending (AHA). Northwell CEO John D'Angelo, an emergency physician himself, says about 400 employees fight insurance denials full time and overturn more than 90% of them — pure labor cost in what he calls \"a 1% margin business at best.\" Highmark says 78% of approved outpatient prior authorizations submitted digitally are already processed automatically.

Discount this

The launch carries no performance figure for the product itself. Savings, shorter waits and earlier detection are forward-looking statements by executives and by Google — no measured results, no pricing, no independent evaluation. Two items on the connector list are not actually there yet either: NextGen Healthcare EHR connectivity is \"slated for release in the coming months,\" and Optum Network Eligibility and Coordination of Benefits is in limited release and testing.

Product SukiSuki OS11/16

Suki offers up to 100% of your money back in place of the clinical data it does not have

What

At HLTH 2026, Suki introduced Suki OS, growing its ambient documentation platform upward into an agentic operating system. Fierce Healthcare reports three pillars: a partner ecosystem (Insight Partners plug their intelligence into Suki's patient context; Platform Partners embed Suki's AI into their own products), unified AI agents, and a performance guarantee. The first two agents are Suki Prior Authorization (identifying requirements and gathering supporting clinical documentation) and a CDI Agent (finding documentation gaps, improving accuracy, supporting coding and revenue cycle work). The company figures: more than 450 healthcare organizations, a $90 million Series E in October, more than $250 million raised in total, encounters supported by its tools up more than 190% year over year in 2026, and revenue doubled in 2025 with similar growth expected this year. Founder and CEO Punit Soni's framing is that healthcare has an \"execution problem\" rather than an AI shortage, and that \"understanding is only the beginning.\"

Why it matters

The Suki Guarantee is the most interesting thing anyone shipped this week, because it moves the burden of proof: eligible customers can get back up to 100% of fees if agreed performance targets are not met. In a market carpeted with \"up to 36%\" and \"6.5% lift\" and no methodology, a vendor willing to put money behind a target is conceding that buyers have stopped believing self-reported numbers. Worth holding alongside that: Suki also appears on Gemini for Healthcare's application partner list as ambient clinical intelligence — simultaneously a brick in Google's platform and a seller of its own operating system, competing for the same slot.

Discount this

The guarantee's terms are not published: which targets, measured how, who counts as an eligible customer, how the cap is computed — none of it. The coverage says Suki has \"independently validated clinical and financial outcomes\" but names not one of them, and names no customer either. The 190% encounter growth and the doubled revenue are company-reported and unaudited.

Distribution AbridgeNACHCAssistant11/13

Abridge gives its Assistant free to 108,000 community health center clinicians — against a rival that is already free

What

On November 13, Abridge and the National Association of Community Health Centers announced that the Abridge Assistant will be free to clinicians at NACHC member community health centers — about 108,000 people — whether or not their organization uses Abridge's documentation product. Those centers serve roughly 52 million people, nearly one in seven Americans and one in three rural residents. The Assistant answers clinical questions with citations to peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines, covering screening guidelines, treatment options and recommended workups, and supports differential diagnosis, medication comparison and clinical calculators, leaving the final decision with the clinician. Abridge says it was founded in 2018, expects its platform to support more than 120 million patient-clinician encounters this year across more than 300 US health systems, has validated encounters in 28+ languages, and won Best in KLAS for ambient AI in both 2025 and 2026. Three days later, on November 16, Wiley announced a partnership with Abridge to bring the evidence behind global clinical guidelines into the Assistant.

Why it matters

This is a distribution land-grab, not charity. Abridge's core business is ambient documentation; the Assistant is clinical question-answering, a category whose incumbent is OpenEvidence — free to all physicians and funded by pharmaceutical and device advertising, with reportedly about 760,000 registered US physicians by December 2025, more than 40% of American doctors. To take users from something already free, you have to be free too, and arrive carrying something the incumbent lacks: in Abridge's case, the pipeline it already runs at the bedside, plus Wiley's guideline evidence. Community health centers are the right beachhead — the tightest budgets, the least likely to buy clinical Q&A on their own — and once clinicians are asking questions inside the same interface, selling them the documentation product is downhill work.

Discount this

No term length is stated and no other financial terms are given. The release names no individual health center and provides no usage or outcome data for the Assistant at all. The 120 million encounters is a projection for this year, not a realized figure.

Inpatient Ambience HealthcareCare Orchestration11/17

Ambience pushes agents into rounding and discharge, self-reporting a 6.5% CMI lift with no methodology attached

What

On November 17, Ambience Healthcare added inpatient care orchestration to its Chorus platform, stepping past documentation into bedside workflow coordination on what it says is two years of inpatient AI development and deployment. Two engines: the Rounding Assistant, which at the start of each shift combines overnight events, physiological changes, lab updates and the hospital course into a prioritized list of suggested clinical tasks; and Discharge Readiness, which runs throughout the admission flagging clinical, operational and social barriers to discharge — pending post-acute placement, durable medical equipment needs, medication access problems — and alerting case managers, social workers and physicians early enough for discharge planning to begin before clinical stability. Both sit on existing tools: the History and Physical Generator, Conditions Advisor, Copy-Forward Intelligence and Discharge Summary Generator. On numbers: a 6.5% case mix index lift at Onvida Health, and an average partner return on investment of about 3x, attributed to better coding accuracy and operational efficiency.

Why it matters

Case mix index is the most honest metric choice of the week, because it converts directly into money: a higher CMI means higher inpatient payment weights, which a hospital CFO understands and will buy. It also shows the inpatient AI business model has settled — what is being sold is not physician time but coding and revenue cycle recovery. Ambience also mentions outcome-aligned commercial fee structures, pointing the same way as Suki's refund guarantee: vendors are starting to tie their own pay to something the buyer can measure.

Discount this

The 6.5% comes with no methodology, no sample size, no time period and no independent verification, and Onvida Health is the only named customer. A second figure in circulation — \"59% more diagnoses (Vizient)\" — appears only in the article's meta description with no context in the body, and this report does not treat it as an established fact. The ~3x ROI is likewise a vendor-reported average with no distribution and no denominator.

M&A CommureElaborate11/16

Commure buys Elaborate, pushing agents into the post-visit inbox

What

On November 16, Commure announced it had acquired Elaborate on undisclosed terms. Elaborate describes itself as a clinical intelligence platform for the clinician inbox, working inside the EHR and following protocols each organization's own clinicians control: helping patients understand their care before they send a message, routing incoming messages to the right care team member, and automating clinically informed workflows. Elaborate's self-reported figures are up to 36% less time in the inbox, a 15% drop in patient message volume, and 97% clinician alignment with the automation; the background figure is that patient portal messages per patient rose 153% from 2020 to 2025, far outpacing growth in office visits. Commure's scale: tens of millions of appointments a year, live in 500+ healthcare organizations including two of the largest for-profit hospital systems in the US, 500,000+ users, and about 7% of American healthcare appointments, with HCA Healthcare named as a user. Co-founder and CEO Tanay Tandon says the combined agents \"can impact tens of thousands of active clinicians on the platform from day one.\"

Why it matters

The inbox is the next plot of land for ambient AI vendors, for a simple reason: ambient documentation solves the fifteen minutes inside the visit, while post-visit messages, result follow-up and referrals are what actually eat a clinician's evening — and it comes with a beautiful growth curve (+153%) to sell against. Commure sits opposite Google here: it is not reading the chart from outside but is already inside the EHR and inside 7% of US appointments, so what it bought was a capability, not distribution. Also heading for the inbox this week: OpenAI's computer-use agent work with Abridge.

Discount this

The price is undisclosed, so there is no telling whether this was a talent buy or a scale buy. The 36%, 15% and 97% all come from the press release with no independent verification, and \"up to\" means best case rather than average. The ~7% of US appointments is also a company figure.

Regulatory GE HealthCareCritical Care Suite 3.0FDA 510(k)11/17

The week's only regulator-checked numbers: GE's feeding-tube AI clears 510(k), running on the x-ray unit itself

What

GE HealthCare's Critical Care Suite 3.0 received US FDA 510(k) clearance (reported by AuntMinnie on November 17; the report gives no separate clearance date or number). It detects enteric feeding tubes, identifies the tube path, tip and side port, and highlights the airways and diaphragm as anatomical reference points to support assessment of tube placement. It runs directly on GE's mobile x-ray systems and, per the vendor, needs no internet or cloud connectivity. It is indicated for adult and pediatric patients — the first Critical Care Suite release in which the feeding tube algorithm supports pediatrics. Vendor-reported performance against expert radiologist annotations: 99.2% tube detection sensitivity and a 91.6% mean Dice score for localization. The software also bundles GE's Quality Care Suite algorithms for reducing image quality errors.

Why it matters

Set this beside the week's other six items and the difference is plain. This is a feature narrow to the point of dullness — where is the tube — but it has a defined population, a performance metric against expert annotations, a completed regulatory pathway, and on-device execution, so it still works in an ICU with no network and on overnight mobile x-ray rounds. Not one of today's \"agentic operating systems\" can produce any of those three things. A misplaced feeding tube is a fatal event, and pediatric support is genuinely new here — which is also why this needs a 510(k) and records synthesis does not.

Discount this

Both 99.2% and 91.6% are vendor-reported against expert radiologist annotations; the coverage gives no test set size, no population composition, no site count, and no independent prospective clinical validation. A 510(k) is a substantial-equivalence clearance, not proof of benefit — it says the device is as safe and effective as an existing one, not that it improves patient outcomes.

Research AppleOpenEvidenceHealth AI Usage Study11/15

Apple will study how 200,000 people ask AI about their health, with OpenEvidence as the clinical engine

What

On November 15, Apple launched the Health AI Usage Study: a virtual study and companion app examining how people use conversational health AI, measuring what affects trust, response quality, comprehension and everyday health actions. Up to 200,000 participants, US only, aged 21 or older, requiring an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone (15 Pro or later) on iOS 26 or later. The clinical partner is OpenEvidence, whose answers draw on medical literature including the New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature; Apple supplied the research design. Participants can ask everyday health questions in plainer language, see linked peer-reviewed citations behind each response, and optionally share HealthKit data — resting heart rate, activity, sleep, lab results — to see how personal data changes relevance and understanding. On privacy: study data will not be used to train AI models, participants control what they share, and guardrails are meant to detect certain concerning questions and point to a provider or other resources. Apple VP of Health and Fitness Sumbul Desai and UCSF chair of medicine Robert M. Wachter are quoted, the latter noting that patients and clinicians are using AI tools \"being built and tested in separate worlds.\"

Why it matters

What matters here is that patient-facing health AI becomes an object of study rather than a commercial black box. But the sharper read is what it means for OpenEvidence, until now a physician-facing product: reportedly valued at $15 billion after a roughly $250 million round in September 2026 (about 50 times annualised revenue), following a $250 million Series D at $12 billion in January 2026 and a $75 million Series A at $1 billion in February 2025; on usage, about 760,000 registered US physicians and some 18 million clinical consultations in December 2025, with a single day passing one million consultations on March 10, 2026. It is free to doctors and paid for by pharmaceutical and device advertising. Now it is also the clinical engine inside an Apple study — while Google wired Optum EvidenceVue, ASCO and BMJ into Gemini for Healthcare. The same slot, cited clinical answers, is being contested from three directions at once.

Discount this

The study has only just opened enrollment and has no results. OpenEvidence's revenue figures are Sacra estimates rather than company disclosures, and the $15 billion valuation is reported rather than confirmed; \"more than 40% of US doctors\" is a December 2025 figure. Note also that the study's sample is skewed by construction: it can only reach US adults who own an iPhone 15 Pro or better and are willing to install a research app.

02 — Product Analysis

Two bets pointing opposite ways: Google wagers connector breadth and gives up the last mile; Suki wagers risk-bearing from the smallest scale. Both share the same unanswered question — who does the measuring

Gemini for Healthcare

Horizontal orchestration layer · Google Cloud (US)

Function and position. Not a system of record but a read-and-orchestrate layer above one, sold to providers and health plans. Five preset skills handle records synthesis, pre-appointment prep, coverage and policy review and payer benefit determination; connectors reach down into Epic (open.epic APIs) and Oracle Health (Ignite FHIR APIs), sideways into InterSystems FHIR Server, Keragon's 400+ applications and Verato's patient identity matching, plus revenue cycle systems Waystar, Cognizant Trizetto, GoodRx and Infinitus Relay, and into the literature via Optum EvidenceVue, ASCO guidelines and BMJ Digital Health (source). Patient records, prompts and outputs stay in the customer's own enterprise environment, and Google states it does not use customer data to train its foundation models.

  • Strength : The breadth is not something a rival can assemble quickly: one launch carrying seven named customers (HCA, Optum and Northwell among them), sixteen systems integrators (Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC, TCS, Wipro and more), and Google, Anthropic and OpenAI models side by side under smart routing (connector and partner list).
  • Strength : The citation discipline is in the right place: answers are grounded in verified charts and approved guidelines with sources attached, agents carry distinct identities, inherit users' role-based permissions, and fall under audit logging and central policy management. The 35-second reconciliation Fortune watched is an unusually concrete, re-testable description for this class of product.
  • Concern : It cannot write to the chart, so it can only ever suggest, never finish. Anything that must land as an order, a schedule or a charge still needs a human for the last mile — and that last mile is where most of the time saving lives. It also means that the moment Epic or Oracle build the same skills natively, the value of this layer compresses.
  • Concern : Zero performance figures. Every quantitative claim in the launch material describes the problem — 28 hours a week on admin, 41.9% burnout, 25–35% administrative share — and none describes the product. No price is published either, so preview customers are making a procurement decision with neither a unit cost nor an effect estimate.
  • Concern : Two connectors are not there yet (NextGen EHR connectivity is \"in the coming months,\" Optum eligibility and coordination of benefits is in limited release and testing), and routing tasks across three vendors' models makes \"why does this answer look like this\" harder to trace in a clinical setting.

Suki OS

Vertical agentic operating system · Suki (US)

Function and position. An operating system grown upward out of ambient documentation, sold to organizations already running ambient tools. Three pillars: a partner ecosystem (Insight Partners feeding intelligence into Suki's patient context, Platform Partners embedding Suki's AI in their own products), unified AI agents, and a performance guarantee. The first agents are Suki Prior Authorization and a CDI Agent, with clinical, operational and financial workflows planned, all designed to run complex tasks under human oversight (source). Company scale: 450+ organizations, a $90M Series E in October, $250M+ raised in total, encounters up more than 190% year over year in 2026.

  • Strength : The Suki Guarantee is the only structurally new thing anyone shipped this week: eligible customers can recover up to 100% of fees if agreed performance targets are missed (Fierce Healthcare). It moves risk back from buyer to seller, and it is the most direct answer anyone has given to this market's self-reported-numbers problem.
  • Strength : The starting point is well chosen. Prior authorization and CDI are cash-flow items, not experience items — the AMA puts prior auth at about 40 per physician per week, roughly 13 hours of physician and staff time (as compiled by Fortune) — and CDI connects straight to coding and payment weights. Growing into both from an existing ambient capture pipeline means the data is already there.
  • Concern : The guarantee's terms are entirely unpublished: which targets, measured how, who qualifies, how the cap is calculated. A guarantee without terms is not a guarantee in procurement; the buyer has to force an auditable measurement definition into the contract or it is marketing language.
  • Concern : \"Independently validated clinical and financial outcomes\" appears in the company's framing, but the coverage lists not one and names no customer — a gap that stands out in a week when Ambience at least named Onvida Health and Commure at least named HCA.
  • Concern : It is simultaneously a Google application partner (listed as ambient clinical intelligence on the Gemini for Healthcare roster) and a seller of its own operating system, while Google's smart routing and five preset skills eat the same orchestration value. Microsoft, Abridge, Ambience and Commure are on this road too, and 450 organizations is not large against that denominator.

04 — Taiwan Angle

What every product above is missing is the one thing Taiwan built an institution to produce

(1) Taiwan turned \"who does the measuring\" into an institution rather than a vendor's volunteer act. Since October 7, 2024 the Ministry of Health and Welfare has run three AI centers (MOHW announcement, center site): a Responsible AI Implementation Center covering security and privacy rules, disclosure of models, data and performance, explainability analysis and AI model lifecycle management; a Clinical AI Validation Center that forms a hospital consortium and links EHR data across care levels to help vendors validate models; and an AI Impact Research Center that builds cross-system clinical trial machinery to assess clinical benefit and establish a \"scientific basis for pricing\" in pursuit of National Health Insurance coverage. In the call for proposals, 30 hospitals submitted 48 proposals and, after three rounds, 19 proposals from 16 hospitals were selected (NTU Hospital, Taichung and Taipei Veterans General, NCKU Hospital, China Medical University Hospital, Tri-Service General and Linkou Chang Gung among them). Read against today's news: Suki has to fund its own guarantee and Ambience can only speak through one customer's CMI, while Taiwan's design starts from the assumption that vendor self-reporting is not credible and builds a third party to do the measuring.

(2) That reimbursement path runs on a three-year clock, not a launch event. The AI Impact Research Center held its joint launch and international forum on November 20, 2025 (UDN) under the theme of advancing AI medical innovation through clinical evidence, with five medical centers — Taipei Veterans General, Taichung Veterans General, Tri-Service General, NCKU Hospital and NTU Hospital — running domestic trials and health technology assessment, officials noting that past evaluations leaned on overseas RCT data. Trial data from the five goes to the NHI Administration's HTA evaluation center and then to the NHI Joint Drafting Committee, whose members include experts, scholars and public representatives, which decides on coverage; MOHW Information Department director Lee Chien-chang expects submission in about three years, noting that drugs and services once covered are rarely removed, so trial design has to be careful. (3) Gemini for Healthcare's moat barely exists in Taiwan, because the layer beneath it is not finished. The entire value of Google's product rests on connectors — Epic's open.epic APIs, Oracle Health's Ignite FHIR APIs. Most Taiwanese hospitals run self-built HIS, so neither connector reaches anything here, which makes data standardization the real precondition: the MOHW's \"333\" policy is rolling out a FHIR Box format with the goal of nationwide interoperability of medical center records by the end of 2026, extending to regional and district hospitals in 2027 and 2028, with a standardized public template for clinics and health centers (UDN, 2026-06-27; the Healthy Taiwan program carries NT$48.9 billion over five years, with a second phase from 2027). Put differently: the US is fighting over the layer above the chart while most institutions here are still laying the layer between charts. That is an opening for local teams — the orchestration fight has already demonstrated that connector breadth decides it, and in Taiwan whoever first gets the cross-institution read layer right stands where Google stands in the US.

(4) The write/no-write line will show up in Taiwan as a guideline question first. The MOHW commissioned Taipei Medical University to help draft a guideline for medical institutions using generative AI, with the plan at the time being to convene the relevant agencies in the first half of 2026 to settle it; until it takes effect hospitals may still pilot AI medical services provided they protect patient data and manage cybersecurity risk, and Department of Medical Affairs deputy director Liu Yu-ching said the guideline is meant to control AI risk and protect patient safety (PTS News, 2025-12-07). Google's read-only choice is a commercial and liability calculation; in Taiwan that line will likely be drawn by guideline rather than by vendor preference. Note that this report is older (December 2025) and this daily has not obtained an updated source on the guideline's final content or timing, so nothing about the current state should be inferred from it.

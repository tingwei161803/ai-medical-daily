Tuesday · Industry & Business

The platform layer holds its ownership, the buyers put up capital, and the only new model arrives with in-house benchmarks

Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · Covering Nov 17–24 · Taipei time

Almost none of this week's medical-AI business news was about models. It was about who owns the records, who pays, and who decides what gets to connect. On 11/19 Oracle CEO Mike Sicilia told the House Veterans' Affairs Committee that the Oracle Health EHR business will stay under Oracle's ownership through 2031 (DistilINFO, 2026-11-23). On 11/18 Epic answered STAT's anti-competitive-practices investigation directly for the first time, from an HLTH stage, promising to make it easier for outside AI to read the records it holds on more than 280 million Americans (STAT, 2026-11-18). The buyers put capital on the table in the same week: Mayo Clinic's CEO-elect placed the Microsoft-built Frontier Health Model inside a $10 billion investment (KIMT, 2026-11-20). And the one new model that raised new money — Knit Health's Med-BEAM, a $35 million Series A — arrived with a fourfold accuracy claim, 100× speed and 250× cost advantage, all from an internal benchmark that does not even name the models it beat (HTWorld, 2026-11-20).

01 — Top Stories

Ownership, access, capital expenditure: all eight of this week's stories land in those three boxes
Ownership Oracle HealthCerner / VA11/19

Oracle's CEO tells Congress: the Oracle Health EHR business stays through 2031, with no advisers hired to weigh a sale

What

Testifying before the House Veterans' Affairs Committee on 11/19, Oracle CEO Mike Sicilia said the Oracle Health EHR business will remain under Oracle's ownership through 2031, and that the company has retained no outside advisers to evaluate a sale, spinoff or restructuring — while conceding that a public company must consider legitimate approaches. The business came out of the roughly $28.3 billion purchase of Cerner in 2022. On 8/18 the VA contract ceiling rose from about $10 billion to nearly $27 billion — some $16.9 billion added, against the roughly $18.5 billion Oracle had proposed — with the term extended to 2031. As of November 2026 the Oracle-based EHR was live at 19 of about 170 VA medical centres, full deployment targeted for 2031 (DistilINFO, 2026-11-23).

Why it matters

Every medical-AI business model ultimately rests on the EHR layer: whose data a model may read, whose fields it writes back to, who gets to charge for it. Oracle Health has been the subject of recurring sale chatter since a January 2026 investment research note, and if an asset contracted to run the US veterans' system through 2031 changed hands, every AI vendor built on top of it would have to renegotiate. Sicilia's words push that uncertainty out five years — while he himself framed it as current management's intent, not a legal restriction.

Discount this

"Through 2031" is management intent, not a contractual obligation, and Sicilia himself left the door open to buyer approaches. Separately, Oracle said in June 2026 that a new AI-powered version of the Cerner EHR would support double-digit Oracle Health revenue growth in fiscal 2027 — a forward-looking expectation, not a realised figure.

Access Epic SystemsHLTH 202611/18

Epic answers the anti-competition investigation at HLTH: it will make it easier for outside AI to read records on 280 million people

What

On a STAT-hosted panel at the HLTH conference in Las Vegas, Epic executives responded directly for the first time to the STAT investigation of 10/28, which found that Epic undercuts competition by selectively withholding the data access rival application developers need. Seth Howard, Epic's executive vice-president of research and development, said the company received about 75,000 inquiries last year from organisations seeking access to its data systems, and that Epic's systems hold records on more than 280 million Americans: "If we built a bespoke connection for every single one of those, that's all we'd ever be [doing]." Epic said it is working toward a more open ecosystem because of the volume of integration requests from third-party vendors, with executives framing their role as letting the free market work (STAT, 2026-11-18).

Why it matters

This is the hardest door in the whole medical-AI startup industry. The shape of it was visible a week earlier, when Google Cloud's Gemini for Healthcare launched with read-only access to Epic and an explicit no-write-back. If Epic genuinely standardises access, the biggest winners are mid-sized vendors with working clinical products stuck in the integration queue. But "more open" is, for now, a verbal commitment with no timeline, no pricing and no API specification.

Discount this

There is nothing verifiable in the commitment: no date, no fee structure, no list of which data classes open up. Both the 75,000 inquiries and the 280 million records are Epic's own figures. The STAT piece is paywalled after its opening section, and this report draws only on the publicly visible portion.

Funding Knit HealthMed-BEAM / NEA11/20

Knit Health's $35M Series A: a healthcare "world model" claims fourfold accuracy without naming what it beat

What

On 11/20 Knit Health — founded in 2025 by University of California, Berkeley scientists — announced a $35 million Series A led by New Enterprise Associates, with Town Hall Ventures, SignalFire and existing investors joining, taking total funding to $46.6 million. Its Med-BEAM is positioned as a healthcare "world model" that analyses medical records and clinical decisions to predict what care a patient may require and to assess how interventions might affect outcomes; the company says it was trained on billions of healthcare decisions and hundreds of millions of de-identified patient records. Stated uses include care management, patient navigation, clinical trial recruitment, specialist scheduling, hospital-at-home and predicting treatment response (HTWorld, 2026-11-20).

Why it matters

This was the week's only deal in which a new model took new money, and its chosen problem sits upstream of what CB Insights counted 26 of its 50 companies doing: not writing the physician's note, but predicting what the patient needs next and then wiring scheduling, recruitment and navigation to that prediction. NEA's willingness to lead a Series A this early says the purchasing appetite of payers and large health systems for predictive administrative automation is already priced into venture models.

Discount this

Every performance number is from the company's own benchmark: on a 24-hour inpatient setting, Med-BEAM is claimed to be up to four times more accurate, 100 times faster and 250 times less expensive than the models compared — but the company does not name those models, and the coverage states plainly that no independent validation was provided. The training-data scale (billions of decisions, hundreds of millions of records) is likewise self-reported and unaudited.

Buyer capex Mayo ClinicMicrosoft11/20

Mayo Clinic's CEO-elect: the Frontier Health Model sits inside a $10 billion investment, with 500+ AI models already deployed

What

On Friday 11/20, Dr Matthew R. Callstrom, Mayo Clinic's president and CEO-elect, told a Destination Medical Center luncheon that the Frontier Health Model, built with Microsoft, is part of a broader $10 billion investment, of which $6 billion is planned for Rochester. He said Mayo has developed and deployed more than 500 AI models, among them LymphoVision, which helps distinguish lymphoma types and cuts diagnosis time to "just a couple of days", and a model that predicts atrial fibrillation up to six months before it becomes clinically meaningful from routine electrocardiograms. The Frontier Health Model will combine Mayo's patient data with AI to predict health conditions — "We're building a model based on our data," Callstrom said — and Mayo plans to share its capabilities with healthcare organisations worldwide (KIMT, 2026-11-20).

Why it matters

This is the week's largest sum, and it is not venture money — it is a buyer's own capital expenditure. A large academic health system announcing it will build a foundation model on its own data and then share that model puts it squarely in the vendor's seat. For companies whose pitch is "we will turn your data into a model" — Knit Health, nference — the biggest customer is simultaneously the biggest potential competitor.

Discount this

The $10 billion comes with no time period and no breakdown; the coverage states explicitly that neither was given, and no costs are attached to the future hospital changes it is linked to. The 500 models, the six-month warning and LymphoVision's "couple of days" are all Mayo's own account, with no peer-reviewed citation or third-party evaluation in the report. This is a local television station's write-up of a luncheon speech, not a formal institutional announcement.

Data moat nferenceCedars-Sinai11/20

nference signs a long-term deal with Cedars-Sinai: 5 million-plus patients, a decade of longitudinal records, not one term disclosed

What

On 11/20 nference announced a "long-term strategic collaboration" with Cedars-Sinai to stand up a secure platform for investigators to analyse de-identified, multimodal clinical data, using nference's federated software to protect privacy, harmonise multimodal data and support longitudinal curation with physician adjudication. The scale is more than 5 million patients and over a decade of longitudinal records, refreshed as routine care progresses. nference describes itself as advancing "healthcare superintelligence as the foundational infrastructure layer for biomedical discovery"; its existing long-term strategic partners include Mayo Clinic, Duke Health, Vanderbilt University Medical Center, Emory Healthcare, Banner Health, Mercy Health and Rush. Dr Sumeet Chugh, vice dean and chief artificial intelligence health research officer at Cedars-Sinai, said he expects the collaboration "will provide our clinicians and scientists with new ways to learn" (PR Newswire, 2026-11-20).

Why it matters

nference's partner list is its valuation: Mayo, Duke, Vanderbilt, Emory, Banner, Mercy, Rush — and now Cedars-Sinai. The commercial logic here is the complement of Epic's door: rather than contest ownership of the EHR, stitch data across health systems with a federated architecture so that nobody has to hand their records over. But Mayo announcing its own Frontier Health Model in the same week shows these academic centres do not intend to remain mere data suppliers.

Discount this

This is a press release, not a deal announcement: the exact terms, the duration and any dollar figure are all undisclosed, with nothing on when the platform goes live or what it must produce. The release also does not say when the existing partnerships began or how they relate to this one. "Healthcare superintelligence" is the company's own marketing framing, with no technical specification or benchmark attached.

Market data CB InsightsDigital Health 5011/17

CB Insights' Digital Health 50: $3.5B raised by the cohort, $2B of it in 2026, and over half are automating admin

What

On 11/17 CB Insights, in partnership with HLTH, published its eighth annual Digital Health 50, picking 50 private companies from pre-seed through Series C out of a global pool of more than 50,000 digital health companies. The cohort has raised $3.5 billion in equity funding, $2 billion of it inside 2026 alone; it has disclosed 91 enterprise partnerships since 2025 — partners include Mayo Clinic, Siemens Healthineers and Merck — and employs more than 6,000 people, at a median headcount of 85. The 50 break down as 11 in care delivery, 8 in administrative operations, 8 in diagnostics and imaging, 5 in clinical workflow automation, 5 in patient communication and engagement, 5 in drug discovery and development, 4 in health insurance solutions and 4 in AI infrastructure and governance — and 26 of the 50 work on reducing administrative labour. Among the winners, Forus raised a $150 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, and Tucuvi's voice AI agent LOLA reaches more than 10% of Spain's public hospitals. Counsel, originally a winner, was removed from the final ranking after Oura announced its acquisition on 11/10 (HIT Consultant, 2026-11-17) — a deal worth $625 million (TechCrunch, 2026-11-10).

Why it matters

This is the week's only third-party quantification of where the money is going, and the conclusion is blunt: early-stage digital health capital is overwhelmingly bet on administrative cost, not clinical outcomes. Twenty-six of the 50 on admin, plus 8 in administrative operations and 5 in clinical workflow automation, means most of the wagers are that health systems will pay for keystrokes not typed. Diagnostics and imaging gets 8; drug discovery, 5.

Discount this

This is a ranking, ordered by CB Insights' own Mosaic Score, and inclusion is not evidence of clinical or commercial performance. Performance figures inside the list remain self-reported: Doctronic says physicians agreed with 91% of its AI-recommended prescription renewals, while the same coverage notes regulatory scrutiny of its Utah pilot.

Market risk STAT First OpinionAI bubble11/23

If the AI bubble bursts, financial markets will cull medical AI before hospitals ever test it

What

On 11/23, David Adler, chief scientific officer at the PATHORA Institute of Pathology & Tissue Medicine, wrote in STAT's First Opinion that "a health AI company's market value and the medical value of its technology are two different things." Citing the 10/30 Wall Street sell-off and Anthropic's 11/19 IPO failing to halt the broader tech slide, he argues that if investors pull funding, hospitals lose the chance to evaluate useful tools: "the financial markets will decide which medical AI technologies survive" before hospitals have had the opportunity to test them. His counter-evidence comes from cancer trial screening: a University of Pennsylvania and Emory study found AI improved accuracy in assessing trial eligibility but did not significantly reduce screening time, and an Australian study (Cancer Council Victoria and Monash Health) found AI identified more eligible patients than standard screening yet did not increase trial enrolment, without establishing why. His prescription is not to rescue every struggling company but for hospitals to set success criteria before adoption and keep research data available for independent evaluation even if the vendor disappears (STAT, 2026-11-23).

Why it matters

Read alongside this week's other seven stories, the structure of the week comes clear: the money sits with the platform layer and the buyers, the startups bring their own benchmarks, and the only two third-party results on the table both say "accuracy improved, the process did not." Adler's argument is precisely the binding constraint on every self-reported number this week — set success criteria before you buy, or market volatility will choose for you.

Discount this

This is an opinion piece (First Opinion), not reporting or research. It names no medical-AI company and gives no market value, valuation or funding figure. The author works at a pathology institute and writes from the buyer's and evaluator's side.

02 — Product Analysis

Two bets on building a foundation model from patient records: one a $35 million startup, one a $10 billion hospital

Med-BEAM

A healthcare "world model" predicting what a patient needs next · Knit Health (US)

Function and position. It analyses medical records and clinical decisions to predict the care a patient may require and to assess how interventions might affect outcomes; the company's stated uses are care management, patient navigation, clinical trial recruitment, specialist scheduling, hospital-at-home and predicting treatment response. The buyer is the administrative and care-management budget of payers and large health systems, not the physician's exam room (HTWorld, 2026-11-20).

  • Strength : the problem sits upstream of administrative automation. CB Insights counted 26 of the Digital Health 50 working on cutting administrative labour, most of them processing paperwork after the fact; predicting what is needed next could, in principle, remove that work rather than speed it up (HIT Consultant, 2026-11-17).
  • Strength : the investor mix knows this buyer. NEA leading, alongside value-based-care specialist Town Hall Ventures, implies a channel into payers and health systems rather than a sales organisation built from scratch (HTWorld, 2026-11-20).
  • Concern : none of the performance numbers can be checked. The fourfold accuracy, 100× speed and 250× cost advantage all come from an in-house 24-hour inpatient benchmark with the compared models unnamed and no independent validation. If a model predicting what a patient needs degrades under real-world distribution, the cost of that degradation lands on care decisions, not paperwork.
  • Concern : founded in 2025 with $46.6 million raised in total, it is up against health systems that own the data themselves. Mayo announcing the Frontier Health Model on its own data in the same week is precisely the structural risk for a company like this: the data sits with the customer, and the customer is also building a model.

Frontier Health Model

Predicting health conditions from its own patient data · Mayo Clinic × Microsoft (US)

Function and position. It combines Mayo's patient data with AI to predict health conditions, built jointly with Microsoft as part of a $10 billion investment ($6 billion of it in Rochester). "We're building a model based on our data," Callstrom said, with a plan to share the model's capabilities with healthcare organisations worldwide — a buyer turning into a supplier (KIMT, 2026-11-20).

  • Strength : the data and the deployment channel sit inside one institution. Mayo has developed and deployed more than 500 AI models, among them one predicting atrial fibrillation up to six months ahead from routine electrocardiograms — no access negotiation, no integration queue (KIMT, 2026-11-20).
  • Strength : the funding is insulated from the venture cycle. This week's STAT op-ed is precisely about market volatility culling tools before hospitals test them — and a $10 billion institutional capital programme does not need a next round (STAT, 2026-11-23).
  • Concern : external validity is the structural weakness of this route. How much of a model built on one academic health system's patients survives a move to institutions with different demographics and different laboratory workflows is not addressed anywhere in the announcement, and "sharing with healthcare organisations worldwide" comes with no licensing model, price or validation plan.
  • Concern : every figure is institutional self-report, relayed by local coverage of a luncheon speech rather than a formal announcement or peer-reviewed publication. The $10 billion comes with no period or breakdown, and the 500 models come with no list of which were prospectively validated.

Where the contrast lies. Both bet on the same proposition — that records become predictive models — but their assets sit on opposite sides: Knit Health has the model and neither the data nor the channel; Mayo has the data, the channel and $10 billion, and lacks external validity and verifiable validation. Neither produced a third-party number this week, so for now the only real difference between them is which can survive the next repricing.

It is simultaneously an existing long-term partner of nference (PR Newswire, 2026-11-20). The moat is data plus brand plus its own deployment channel, with capital expenditure independent of the venture cycle. It is thin on external validity and on any licensing mechanism — and it is simultaneously its suppliers' customer, partner and competitor.

Federated multimodal clinical data infrastructure		 On 11/20 it signed an undisclosed-terms long-term strategic collaboration with Cedars-Sinai covering more than 5 million patients and over a decade of longitudinal records; existing partners include Mayo Clinic, Duke Health, Vanderbilt, Emory, Banner Health, Mercy Health and Rush (PR Newswire, 2026-11-20). Knit Health
Healthcare world-model startup, founded 2025		 On 11/20 it closed a $35 million Series A led by NEA, $46.6 million in total; Med-BEAM claims up to fourfold accuracy, 100× speed and 250× lower cost on an internal 24-hour inpatient benchmark, with the compared models unnamed and no independent validation (HTWorld, 2026-11-20). For now the moat is the model and the team (out of Berkeley). Oura
Wearables maker expanding into AI primary care		 On 11/10 it announced a $625 million acquisition of AI-native primary care startup Counsel Health (TechCrunch, 2026-11-10; STAT, 2026-11-10). The effect showed up on 11/17: Counsel, an original Digital Health 50 winner, was removed from the final ranking because of the deal (HIT Consultant, 2026-11-17). The moat is an installed base of consumer devices taking continuous physiological measurements, plus an acquired clinical front end. It is thin because crossing from consumer wearables into primary care means regulation and reimbursement rather than a hardware supply chain, and no terms were published on what has to happen for the $625 million to pay back.

Today's competitive structure has two layers, not one line. The lower layer is ownership and access — Oracle locks the ground to 2031 by contract, Epic controls who gets in through the integration queue, and nference routes around both with a federated architecture. The upper layer is models, and at that layer this week produced no third-party number at all, whether from a $35 million startup or a $10 billion hospital. The real variable is the buyer: once Mayo decides to build its own model and share it outward, a vendor's customer list and its competitor list start to overlap.

04 — Taiwan Angle

Where next Wednesday's Taiwan expo sits inside that structure

(1) Taiwan's show floor is organised around devices and settings, not the records layer. The 2026 Taiwan Medical Technology Expo runs 3–6 December at Hall 1 of the Nangang Exhibition Center. On 11/23 the Qisda Group announced it will exhibit under the theme "AI medical care and health care" with 17 group companies, across four scenarios: AI sports medicine and senior health; smart clinical and in-hospital treatment; the future pharmacy and smart drug delivery; and pet care and home living. The numbers general manager Ke Shu-feng cited are about channel and deployment sites — over a hundred pharmacy locations, a cross-sea AI drone delivery trial between Donggang and Xiaoliuqiu with more than 100 trips and 1,700 km logged, and a pet-care market of roughly 3.2 million dogs and cats nationwide (CNA, 2026-11-23). Set against this week's eight American stories, Taiwanese firms are not competing at Epic's door; they are in the same box as Korea's Mediintech and Hexarobotics — hardware, sites, distribution.

(2) This week's buyer signals are an opening for Taiwan and also its valuation ceiling. Mayo is putting $10 billion of capital expenditure behind building a model on its own data and sharing it with health systems worldwide (KIMT, 2026-11-20), and nference is wiring eight American health systems together without moving a single record (PR Newswire, 2026-11-20). Both routes consume edge compute, image capture and in-hospital devices — things Taiwan's supply chain sells. But the ceiling is equally clear: in this structure the bargaining power over models and data sits nowhere near the device maker, and of the $3.5 billion in early-stage capital CB Insights counted, more than half of the 50 companies are writing administrative software, not building hardware (HIT Consultant, 2026-11-17).

(3) Europe's show floor has already changed the subject to regulation. MEDICA 2026 ran 16–19 November in Düsseldorf, with more than 700 speakers across MEDICA and COMPAMED, a full second day given to AI in diagnostics, and "AI in Diagnostics & Imaging" among its hot topics (MEDICA). The advice to manufacturers on the floor was that ongoing revisions to the EU MDR and the AI Act require close attention (Medical Device Network, 2026-11-23). For Taiwanese firms hoping to sell December's exhibits into Europe, the first problem is not model performance but the intersection of those two regimes.

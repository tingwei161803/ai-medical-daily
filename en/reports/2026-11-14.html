Governance NVIDIA Colette Kress 11/12

Nvidia's board announces Huang's resignation as CEO and director; the CFO steps in and the company gives no reason

What

On Nov 12 Nvidia's board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and board member with immediate effect, ending a 33-year run as CEO, with CFO Colette Kress as interim chief executive and Harvey Jones as board chair; Al Jazeera notes the company gave no reason for the departure. Huang had admitted the previous Sunday to being unfaithful to his wife and apologised to his family and employees, and ABC News reports the board opened an independent investigation after he acknowledged a relationship with a Chinese business associate. The share reaction belongs to two different windows: Mirror Media records a 14% intraday fall on Nov 12 and a 4.34% close, while ABC separately notes the stock had already lost at least 24% over the previous month, about US$1.4tn of market value, from roughly US$4tn before the decline. Kress has been CFO since September 2013, over which Nvidia's annual revenue grew from US$4.13bn in fiscal 2014 to US$215.9bn in fiscal 2026; Jones said he expects operations, customer commitments and the product road map to continue without interruption.

Why it matters

This is a capital-structure event, not only a personnel one. Economic Daily News sets out three transmission paths: besides selling chips, Huang backed AI labs and cloud builders through equity investments, chip credit lines and financing guarantees, and Bernstein's analyst asks directly what becomes of those investments, that financing support and the residual-value guarantees. If the new management turns conservative, what moves first is customers' funding and build-out plans, and only then demand for chips, advanced packaging and servers. The relevance to medical AI is direct: inference costs at the hospital end have fallen for two years on the assumption that a build-out financed by a single vendor acting as both seller and lender keeps running. What was shaken this week is that assumption, not any medical model.

Discount this

Nvidia gave no official reason. The Chinese-intelligence thread is a New York Times report of a US intelligence conclusion, denied by Beijing, with Nvidia saying it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised. A proposed class action alleging that Nvidia and Huang defrauded shareholders by concealing the relationship is an allegation, not a finding, and House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast's statement that he expects testimony within about a week is an expectation rather than a scheduled hearing (ABC News). The 14% intraday and the 4.34% close are different windows and must not be mixed, and the US$500bn and US$1.4tn figures for lost market value come from different measurement periods.

Genomics Google DeepMind AlphaGenome 2 11/11

AlphaGenome 2 pushes variant interpretation from tissue level to cell state — and rules out clinical use in its own announcement

What

Google DeepMind published AlphaGenome 2 (authors Adam Gayoso, Natasha Latysheva, Pushmeet Kohli and Žiga Avsec). Where the previous generation emitted fixed parallel outputs for bulk tissues and cell lines, this one is conditional: it takes a biological condition — a tissue, cell type or state — plus about one million DNA letters, and predicts one condition at a time. Readouts cover gene expression, histone modifications and chromatin accessibility, plus DNA methylation, which the previous model did not predict; training draws on bulk and single-cell human and mouse data, over 107 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues (DeepMind Blog). On TenK10K's 28 primary immune cell types it identifies causal variants in 30% of cases against 16% for the original AlphaGenome fine-tuned on single-cell data, and directional gene effects 43% against 23%. It also reproduced computationally a published finding that a variant in a distant regulatory switch overactivates ETS2 in gut macrophages, linked to inflammatory bowel disease. Access is from today for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, with a private preview for commercial research and a feedback form closing Nov 30.

Why it matters

GWAS hits have been stuck in the same place for years: the signal sits in non-coding regions, and tissue-level models cannot separate neighbouring cell types within one tissue. The substantive change here is the cell encoder — researchers can condition the model on their own single-cell data, and DeepMind says that starting from a single-cell RNA-seq snapshot of a few dozen cells, with no retraining, it matches or outperforms models trained or fine-tuned directly on those datasets; Human Cell Atlas co-founder Sarah Teichmann's phrasing is that it "adapts directly to new single-cell data without retraining." Cross-modality imputation is the other practical face: given one measurement, such as single-cell gene activity, it predicts an unmeasured property such as chromatin accessibility — which changes the cost structure, not the accuracy, for a lab that can only afford one assay.

Discount this

The announcement itself states the model is for research use only and is not validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic purposes, and that directional predictions are reported only where the model is confident. The baselines are the previous AlphaGenome fine-tuned on single-cell data and "other specialised tools", chosen and run by the publisher rather than in an independent benchmark, and the post's own introduction says "more than 100 million" profiles where the detail says 107 million. The licence matters too: only non-commercial research is open today, with commercial research in private preview.

Embodied AI Google DeepMind Gemini Robotics 2.5 11/10

Gemini Robotics 2.5 lists pipetting among its tasks, and the per-task numbers show where the ceiling still is

What

On Nov 10 DeepMind published Gemini Robotics 2.5, described as its most capable vision-language-action model to date, evaluated across more than 200 distinct tasks — a list that includes serving food with tongs, tying a bow on a gift box, and pipetting liquid. Success rates are 30% to 100% higher than the previous generation depending on task type, with tasks completed 20–30% faster. The per-task figures show the shape better: closing the lid of a black toolbox went 0%→93%, packing pastries with tongs 5%→100%, untying ribbon bows 0%→70%, hanging a cardigan 0%→62%, folding pants 0%→40% (DeepMind Blog). The field component is with Apptronik and Mercedes-Benz, using the Apollo 2 humanoid for automotive kitting on active production lines in Kecskemét, Hungary and Sindelfingen, Germany, at a 96% proof-of-concept success rate, with Gemini Robotics ER tracking the workflow and 2.5 controlling the arms. On safety, inference-time reasoning guidance raised safe task completion by 33% around obstacles and 43% around nearby people, and the team open-sourced ASIMOV-Jailbreak on Hugging Face, where Gemini 4 Argon resists 99.5% of attempts against 82% for the previous baseline, Gemini Robotics ER 2.

Why it matters

Medicine's nearest contact point here is not the ward but the bench. Pipetting appearing on the task list means models of this class are starting to touch the most repetitive, most error-prone and most heavily regulated motions in laboratory and research workflows — while folding pants at 0%→40% says generalisable dexterity is nowhere near hands-off. What is most worth copying is how the safety numbers are reported: 33% and 43% are given per scenario rather than as one aggregate, and DeepMind open-sourced the jailbreak benchmark so third parties can rerun the same prompts — which is precisely what the week's other releases did not do.

Discount this

Rollout is to robotics partners only — Agile Robots SE, Apptronik and Boston Dynamics among them — with no broader availability mentioned on the official page. The 96% is a Google and Apptronik proof-of-concept figure, not an audited production yield, and "30–100% higher" spans several tasks where the previous model scored 0%, so the denominator is zero and the range reads better than the capability jump it actually represents.

Agentic commerce OpenAI ChatGPT Wallet 11/13

ChatGPT Wallet lets agents check out — and OpenAI itself says spending limits are not yet available

What

On Nov 13 OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, letting its agents — called dots — help decide what to buy and then complete the purchase, with OpenAI saying users are "fully in control" of those purchases. Mechanically: a user adds eligible cards to Wallet once, then asks a dot to buy; before checkout they review the items, the total and the card, and approve each purchase with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint, which creates a transaction record. Wallet charges the card through a payment token, so card details never reach the dot or the merchant, while the dot passes shipping and contact details to the merchant; card rewards and protections such as transaction disputes are preserved (TechCrunch). The rollout is deliberately narrow: a restricted number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers, aged 18 and over, with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers wanting to support agent shopping.

Why it matters

Today's consent model is a biometric approval on every transaction, which is strict. The real signal is the next sentence: TechCrunch notes that spending limits, and rules by purchase category or time period, are features intended for pre-approved autonomous buying — and that those controls are not yet available. The roadmap has written down its destination, which is purchasing without per-transaction approval, and the guardrails have not arrived. The medical analogue has the same shape: procurement, consumables restocking, even self-pay checkout are low-value, high-frequency, clearly-ruled work, exactly the class that gets delegated first — and what decides whether it can be is not model accuracy but whether limits and category rules exist at all.

Discount this

No participating merchants are named. TechCrunch notes DoorDash and Uber report low agent-placed order volumes, while Amazon blocks AI agents to steer users toward its own agent, Alexa — so reach is unproven. OpenAI frames the limited launch as a way to test and improve the feature rather than a general release.

Productivity Anthropic Claude Meetings 11/10

Claude joins the video call, and "leaves automatically if an external guest joins" is the week's one fail-closed consent default

What

On Nov 10 Anthropic launched Claude Meetings, Whiteboards and Sheets. Meetings puts Claude into video calls as a visible participant that follows the conversation and works in real time — drafting the plan under discussion, mapping a timeline on a whiteboard, answering questions in the meeting chat and pulling from connected work apps, with notes, action items and materials available in claude.ai afterwards. The permission design is the point of this item: Claude identifies itself as a participant and posts a notice in the meeting chat, will not join calls with external guests unless an admin authorises it, and leaves automatically if an external guest joins (TechCrunch). Meetings is in beta on Team and Enterprise plans, with Google Meet supported at launch; Whiteboards and Sheets are in beta on paid plans across web, desktop and mobile. Claude Data, formerly Claude Dashboards, is now generally available, connecting to data warehouses and CRMs and answering with dashboards, written data stories or narrated videos, while interactive connectors open full versions of Adobe, Atlassian and HubSpot inside conversations.

Why it matters

Three products this week handed more authority to agents, and only this one defaults to failing closed: faced with an unauthorised external participant, the behaviour is to leave rather than to continue and log. For a hospital's security and compliance function, "leaves automatically if an external guest joins" is a specification that can be written into an audit clause and verified; "users are fully in control" is not. The clinical analogue is plain — cross-institution meetings, discussions with a pharma or vendor representative on the line, a family member joining a video call — and what decides whether this is usable was never whether the model can take minutes, but what it does when there is one more person in the room than there should be.

Discount this

Meetings, Whiteboards and Sheets are all in beta, with Google Meet the only supported platform at launch. TechCrunch's report gives no pricing, user counts or revenue figures, and no implementation detail or third-party verification for the permission rules above; the visible-participant behaviour and the automatic exit are both vendor-described.

Evaluation arXiv KDD 2026 11/10

A KDD 2026 paper measures the gap in agent evaluation: functional testing and security red-teaming found novel failures with zero overlap

What

arXiv preprint 2611.14493v1, posted Nov 10 (authors Howard Zhang, Chuck Arvin, Logan Brown, Mihir Bhatia, Neelu Choudhary and Lily Miao; 9 pages, 3 figures; accepted to KDD 2026 in Jeju), compares three evaluation frameworks for LLM-based analytics agents — trace-based LLM judging, text-only LLM judging with red-teaming, and deterministic heuristics — applying them to two agents over frozen execution traces in three phases (arXiv). Phase one, stability: 97% of configurations met the stability threshold, and the stability patterns were agent-independent. Phase two, known-risk detection: across 72 ground-truth test cases the trace-based framework had the best precision-recall balance at F1 0.90, while text-only evaluation matched its recall but produced five times more false alarms. Phase three, unknown-risk discovery: 24 novel failures, with zero overlap between what functional testing found and what security red-teaming found. The authors recommend running two frameworks: trace-based evaluation for function, adversarial red-teaming for security.

Why it matters

The zero overlap is this paper's real finding, and the control group for everything else this week. If functional testing and security red-teaming surface two disjoint sets of failures, then a single evaluation suite — which is what most procurement processes actually ask for — measures roughly half of what it claims to cover. The other directly transferable conclusion is that what the evaluation harness can see determines evaluation quality: the framework with access to execution traces reached F1 0.90, while the one seeing only text output raised five times more false alarms. Put in clinical terms: validating a clinical agent by reading only its final paragraph, rather than which data it called, in what order, and what it changed along the way, measures composition rather than safety.

Discount this

The subject is enterprise analytics agents, not a clinical setting, and only two agents were tested, over frozen traces, with the number of repeated runs not stated. The three frameworks were compared by the authors on their own setup rather than cross-validated independently, and this is a preprint — accepted to KDD 2026, but the final version may still change.

02 — Product Analysis

One week, one question: both products claim to push research productivity forward, and only one of them wrote down its control group.

AlphaGenome 2

Conditional DNA sequence model · Google DeepMind (UK/US)

Function and position. Takes a biological condition plus about one million DNA letters and predicts, for that one condition, gene expression, histone modifications, chromatin accessibility and DNA methylation. The users are human-genetics and single-cell biologists, and delivery is an API rather than a software licence; the DeepMind announcement states access opens today for non-commercial research with commercial research in private preview.

  • Strength : the baselines are named and rerunnable. On TenK10K's 28 primary immune cell types, causal variants 30% against the previous generation's 16% and directional gene effects 43% against 23%, with the comparison stated as the previous model fine-tuned on single-cell data (DeepMind).
  • Strength : it conditions on the user's own single-cell data without retraining, matching models fine-tuned on a dataset from a snapshot of a few dozen cells — which, for a lab with no compute to retrain, is the line between usable and not.
  • Concern : the announcement rules out clinical use itself — research only, not approved for clinical or diagnostic purposes — and directional predictions are reported only where the model is confident, with the confidence threshold not published.
  • Concern : every figure was chosen and run by the publisher, and what is missing is a third party rerunning it on unseen data; the post's "more than 100 million" and its detailed "107 million" single-cell profiles also differ slightly.

Lor / Fern (Mirendil)

Agent-driven AI R&D stack · Mirendil (US)

an class="en-inline">Lor has agents write code, run experiments and iterate for days while provisioning their own compute, currently in private beta; Fern is the first in-house model, pretrained from scratch with a context window up to 10M tokens. The buyers are other AI labs and R&D teams, and the company states its mission as democratising frontier AI R&D (Mirendil).

  • Strength : if the cost claim holds, it does move the threshold — the company says Mirendil-Base was completed by one researcher directing agents over 3.5 months on under US$10m of compute all-in (Tech Times).
  • Strength : the company is measured about its own claims, saying plainly that the model was not built autonomously and has not reached fully autonomous recursive self-improvement — at this narrative density, that restraint is itself a signal.
  • Concern : there is no third-party evaluation. The one quasi-external metric is bits-per-byte on seven external code repositories at 0.222 against Kimi-K3's 0.193 — lower is better, so it is the one behind — and the 10M-token figure rests on a basic RULER test, which overstates long-context ability.
  • Concern : what is missing is not data but guardrails. Lor's agents provision their own compute, and nothing in the public material describes safety checks, sandboxing or spending limits; the investor list overlaps heavily with the compute supplier and the chip vendor.

04 — Taiwan Angle

Three things: where the supply-chain risk actually sits, the gap between agent authority and existing rules, and an API open only to non-commercial research

(1) The supply-chain risk is not capacity — it is order intake and the guarantee arrangements. Several analysts put the direct impact on TSMC low: Counterpoint Research's William Li says there is no sign of Nvidia cutting orders, and Switchyard Partners' Kasser believes TSMC can shift capacity to other customers quickly (PTS News). What is actually moving is procedure — Economic Daily News, citing Nikkei, reports a Taiwanese server executive travelling to Southeast Asia personally to verify that small customers' AI servers are switched on and running, a check that never previously required someone at that level, while another server maker has told sales to stop accepting orders from unfamiliar new and small customers across Nvidia, AMD and Intel products, and Taiwanese firms have tightened customer and destination checks of their own accord before any new US restrictions are published. Taishin Financial Holdings chief economist Lee Chen-yu reads Huang as having held the AI supply chain together through personal standing, with decisions likely to turn conservative; Chris Hampden of Bain's Proxima argues a change of CEO is no basis for inferring a chip squeeze, and that what matters is orders, long-term capacity commitments, sales channels and regulatory change. For a medical-AI team in Taiwan, the actionable indicator is therefore not Nvidia's share price but whether lead times and long-term contract terms for on-premise GPUs change.

(2) The authority being handed to agents already exceeds what Taiwan's generative-AI guidance for healthcare institutions addresses. Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare issued its Guidance on the Use of Generative AI in Healthcare Institutions on 29 May 2026 (ref. 1151663164), centred on patient privacy and the right to be informed: patients must be told in advance where generative AI with recording capability is used, and an AI tool that speaks directly with the public must disclose itself (Taiwan Hospital Association). Measured against that ruler, this week's three products land differently. Claude Meetings' self-identification as a participant and its notice in the meeting chat is close to an engineering implementation of that disclosure principle, with a fail-closed layer on top — no joining calls with external guests without admin authorisation, and leaving as soon as one joins (TechCrunch). ChatGPT Wallet's model — biometric approval per transaction, spending limits not yet available — falls outside the guidance entirely: disclosure and informed consent address whether the patient knows AI is present, not how large a decision an agent may take on an institution's behalf, under what limit, and who can reverse it afterwards. That is a regulatory gap rather than a violation — products of this kind did not exist when the guidance was issued.

(3) For Taiwan, AlphaGenome 2 is a usable research tool, and its licence terms are also its ceiling. The access terms deserve attention: the API opens today for non-commercial research with commercial research in private preview, and the announcement states plainly that the model is research-only and not validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic purposes (DeepMind). The property that matters most — conditioning on your own single-cell data without retraining, matching fine-tuned models from a snapshot of a few dozen cells — is worth most to academic labs without a large compute budget, which is where most of Taiwan's genomics groups sit. To be explicit, what follows is this report's inference rather than anything drawn from the sources: those terms mean that wiring the model into self-pay testing, LDTs or a clinical reporting pipeline does not work under the current licence. What it can do is hypothesis generation and variant prioritisation, still followed by experimental validation — which is the boundary DeepMind drew itself.

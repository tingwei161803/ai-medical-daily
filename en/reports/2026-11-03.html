Tuesday · Industry & Business

AI capital is tightening while medical AI's P&L turns: in the same week OpenAI cut its round from $30B to $20B and its valuation from $1.4T to $1.25T, Tempus turned adjusted EBITDA positive for the first time, Hinge Health grew 52% at an 87% gross margin, and HGP reported private health IT's valuation discount closing for the first time in a decade

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · Covering Oct 26–Nov 3 · Taipei time

Two capital signals pointed in opposite directions this week, three days apart. On Oct 30 the Financial Times reported OpenAI cutting its current round from $30 billion to $20 billion and its valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion; per the Wall Street Journal it has secured roughly $11 billion in commitments and now plans to raise from family offices and high-net-worth individuals. The same day the Nasdaq 100 fell 3.5%, having shed more than $1.2 trillion in market capitalization since early October, and Alphabet dropped 13% in a single session on Oct 29, erasing about $500 billion (PYMNTS, 2026-11-01). Then on Nov 2 two medical AI filings ran the other way: Tempus AI reported $411.5M in quarterly revenue, up 23%, with adjusted EBITDA positive for the first time at $25.6M, and Hinge Health posted $235.0M, up 52%, on an 87% GAAP gross margin with $57.4M of GAAP net income. HGP's Oct 30 monthly snapshot adds that private health IT revenue multiples are back near 5.0x, closing the sector's valuation discount to public enterprise SaaS for the first time since tracking began in 2016. The dividing line is not narrative quality but whether someone pays, how much, and whether the money reaches an existing workflow — on Oct 29 OpenEvidence went straight into NYU Langone's Epic, covering 12.4 million outpatient visits a year.

Earnings Tempus AIData & Applications11/02

Tempus AI posts $411.5M, up 23%, with its first positive adjusted EBITDA at $25.6M, and raises full-year revenue guidance to $1.60–1.605B

What

The Q3 2026 release on Nov 2: total revenue $411.5M, up 23%; gross profit $266.7M, up 27%, with a 66% non-GAAP gross margin against 64% a year earlier; adjusted EBITDA of $25.6M versus just $1.5M in Q3 2025 — the company's first positive quarter; net loss narrowed 81% from $80.0M to $14.9M; non-GAAP net income $13.4M against a $19.6M loss; cash and marketable securities $854.4M. Two segments: Diagnostics $303.5M, up 20% (oncology $172.6M, up 28%; hereditary cancer testing $111.3M), and Data & Applications $108.1M, up 33%, with Insights up 29% and roughly $500M in year-to-date licensing bookings. On volume: oncology units up 32%, 12,000 MRD tests in the quarter (roughly 300% growth), therapy-selection ASP at $1,880 against a target above $2,600; data holdings above 600 petabytes with more than 50 added in the quarter. Guidance: full-year revenue raised to $1.60–1.605B (about 25% growth), full-year adjusted EBITDA held at about $65M, Q4 revenue $458–463M and Q4 adjusted EBITDA about $34M (Investing.com summary of the earnings deck, 2026-11-02).

Why it matters

The question asked of every medical AI company this cycle is when it makes money. Tempus's answer does not run through the model: it runs through pushing test volume up (oncology units +32%) and then selling the same data a second time to pharma (Data & Applications +33%, roughly $500M in year-to-date licensing bookings). Note the structure: Data & Applications is only 26% of revenue yet is the fastest-growing and highest-margin piece, and its raw material comes from the 74% that is Diagnostics. That is a flywheel in which the diagnostics business subsidizes the data business and the data business pulls the blended margin up — hard to copy with a model alone. The pending Personalis acquisition goes to a shareholder vote on Nov 30, which would layer on more MRD and tumor genomic data.

Discount this

The first positive adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP measure; the GAAP net loss is still $14.9M. The company held full-year adjusted EBITDA at about $65M rather than raising it, effectively conceding that the $34M Q4 figure already absorbs this quarter's upside. The MRD comparison of 12,000 against 4,700 tests is labelled in the secondary summary as being against Q4 2025 rather than Q3 2025 — treat the company's own filing as authoritative on the base period. The BMS collaboration's figure of up to $192.5M in Data & Applications revenue over five years is a ceiling, not recognized revenue.

Earnings Hinge HealthNYSE: HNGE11/02

Hinge Health posts $235.0M, up 52%, at an 87% GAAP gross margin with $57.4M of GAAP net income and a 48% free-cash-flow margin — AI-automated care with software-like unit economics

What

The Q3 2026 release on Nov 2: revenue $235.0M against $154.2M a year earlier, up 52%; gross margin 87% GAAP and 88% non-GAAP; GAAP operating income $50.3M (21% margin) and non-GAAP operating income $77.2M (33%); GAAP net income $57.4M; GAAP diluted EPS $0.66 and non-GAAP $0.71; free cash flow $112.7M at a 48% margin; 3,270 clients, up 28% from 2,560; trailing-twelve-month calculated billings $960.3M, up 54%. Guidance raised: full-year revenue $879–881M (about 50% growth at the midpoint), non-GAAP operating income $266–268M (30% margin at the midpoint), and Q4 revenue $249–251M (company release, 2026-11-02).

Why it matters

An 87% gross margin essentially does not exist in healthcare services — it is what happens when labor-intensive musculoskeletal rehab replaces most of its human contact with motion tracking and automated care pathways. It gives the sector a reference number: when AI actually replaces the cost of delivery rather than sitting on top of it, gross margin lands above 85% and free-cash-flow margin approaches half of revenue. Set against Teladoc's $605M declining 3% in the same week, the question is not whether virtual care works but whether delivery is done by people or by machines. One caveat: GAAP net income of $57.4M exceeds GAAP operating income of $50.3M, with the difference coming from non-operating items, so this quarter's net margin should not simply be annualized.

Discount this

The release describes an AI-powered care model that scales and automates care delivery, but publishes no care-delivery automation percentage, and reports no EBITDA (quarterly depreciation and amortization was only $2.2M). Revenue of $235.0M came in slightly below the $239.0M consensus, growth is split between the 28% client increase and rising revenue per client, and neither renewal rate nor net retention in the employer channel is disclosed here.

OpenAI cuts its round from $30B to $20B and its valuation from $1.4T to $1.25T; the Nasdaq 100 falls 3.5% that day, having shed more than $1.2T since early October

What

On Friday Oct 30 the Financial Times reported OpenAI cutting its current round from $30 billion to $20 billion and its valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion; the Wall Street Journal put commitments at roughly $11 billion, with plans to raise the rest from family offices and high-net-worth individuals after some large institutions showed tepid interest. On the market side, the Nasdaq 100 fell 3.5% that day, having lost more than $1.2 trillion in market capitalization since early October, and Alphabet dropped 13% in a single session on Oct 29, erasing about $500 billion. The reporting also names Anthropic's IPO preparations as competing for investor attention; its S-1 disclosed $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, up about 1,300% year on year, against an $80.7 billion net loss (PYMNTS, summarizing FT and WSJ reporting, 2026-11-01).

Why it matters

For medical AI this cuts two ways. The bad: late-stage rounds and IPO pricing in health AI are anchored to general AI multiples, so a markdown at the frontier labs propagates down the same curve — hardest on companies with no revenue raising on a foundation-model-plus-medicine narrative. The good: as capital rotates from compute-for-narrative toward cash-flow-for-valuation, Tempus's first positive EBITDA quarter and Hinge Health's 87% gross margin stop being line items and become some of the few directly comparable hard evidence this cycle offers. It also helps explain what HGP observed in the same month — private health IT multiples back at 5.0x.

Discount this

The round size, valuation and commitment figures all come from unnamed sources cited by the FT and WSJ; OpenAI did not respond to PYMNTS's request for comment and there is no company announcement. This item is written from secondary coverage and the numbers may move with the final round terms. Anthropic's $80.7 billion net loss largely reflects non-cash items including revaluation and is not cash burn; this publication covered its S-1 in detail on Oct 31.

Distribution OpenEvidenceNYU LangoneEpic10/29

OpenEvidence goes live inside NYU Langone's Epic workflow systemwide: 12.4M outpatient visits a year, over 421,000 ED visits, more than 6,000 physicians and faculty, and more than 2,000 residents and fellows

What

On Oct 29 NYU Langone Health and OpenEvidence announced a collaboration embedding OpenEvidence's clinical evidence Q&A directly into Epic: clinicians type a complex clinical question in natural language inside their existing workflow and get back an answer drawn from peer-reviewed literature that accounts for the individual patient's context. The footprint covers the system's seven inpatient locations and more than 400 outpatient sites across the New York region and Florida, 12.4 million outpatient visits a year and more than 421,000 ED visits, with the Family Health Centers serving over 100,000 patients across roughly 600,000 visits. Users include more than 6,000 physicians and faculty, more than 2,000 residents and fellows, and trainees at both NYU Grossman medical schools, within a system of more than 54,000 employees. CDIO Nader Mherabi said the aim is to make the latest technology available to clinicians in a safe and practical environment at scale; founder Daniel Nadler said the world's medical literature is now in the room for every one of those twelve million visits (Unite.AI, 2026-10-29).

Why it matters

This is the week's cleanest case of distribution beating the model. OpenEvidence only pushed its valuation to $15 billion with a $250M Series D in September (Axios, 2026-09-25), and what supports that multiple is not benchmark scores — in the Real Clinical Queries blinded evaluation this publication covered on Oct 29, OpenEvidence scored 62.6 on the 500-item open-ended HealthBench against GPT's 88.0 — but whether it sits inside the screen clinicians already have open. Going into Epic removes the single largest adoption barrier, which is asking a physician to open one more tab. For incumbent clinical decision support such as UpToDate, that is direct substitution at the distribution layer.

Discount this

Neither party disclosed contract value, term or who pays, and no post-launch usage, adoption or clinical outcome figures were published. The 12.4 million visits is NYU Langone's total volume, not OpenEvidence's actual query count — the Jefferson Health data this publication cited on Nov 1 showed 5,234 provisioned accounts yielding only 1,802 actual users. The claim of being the most popular AI tool for US doctors is vendor-stated, with no supporting number in the source.

Market data HGPhealth IT 估值10/30

Private health IT revenue multiples back near 5.0x — HGP says the sector's valuation discount to public enterprise SaaS has closed for the first time since tracking began in 2016; annualized M&A near 420 deals and annualized investment near $13B

What

On Oct 30 HIStalk published its October health tech market snapshot built on Healthcare Growth Partners (HGP) data, covering transactions announced Oct 1–28. On valuation: private health IT revenue multiples have rebounded toward 5.0x, and for the first time since HGP began tracking in 2016 the sector has closed its historical valuation discount to public enterprise SaaS. On volume: annualized M&A has settled near 420 transactions, down from a late-2025 peak. On capital: annualized investment is near $13 billion excluding a single $2.7 billion September financing, with money concentrating in larger rounds. October deals in the same snapshot include Agilent acquiring digital pathology vendor Proscia for $240M upfront plus $30M in milestones (about 15x LTM revenue including the earnout), Zocdoc making its first acquisition in twenty years by buying AI insurance eligibility vendor Sohar Health (undisclosed; Sohar had raised just $3.8M since 2023), General Medicine's $120M Series B led by Andreessen Horowitz (total $152M), Suki's $90M Series E (total $255M), Sirona Medical's $40M Series D at a $253M post-money against $170M in 2025, and Healthleap's $38M combined seed and Series A at a $205.5M post-money (HIStalk / HGP snapshot, 2026-10-30).

Why it matters

This is the structural spine of today's report. For a decade health IT was priced at a healthcare discount: long sales cycles, concentrated customers, procurement trapped inside IT budgets, so the same revenue growth rate bought a lower multiple than enterprise SaaS. The discount closing means buyers now believe healthcare software's renewal and expansion behavior is not fundamentally different from ordinary B2B SaaS. That this happened in the same month general AI valuations were marked down — in the opposite direction — suggests medical AI's repricing is tracking verifiable revenue structure rather than the AI cycle. The Tempus and Hinge Health quarters are the two newest data points in that structure.

Discount this

The 5.0x figure and the closed-discount claim come from HGP's proprietary dataset and methodology, are not independently audited, and the sample and weighting are not published; private transaction multiples carry survivorship bias, since closed deals are recorded and failed processes are not. The annualized figures extrapolate from less than a full year, and a single very large transaction visibly distorts them — the snapshot itself excludes September's $2.7 billion financing for exactly that reason.

02 — Product Analysis

Two ways of selling clinical AI, both in this week's news: one puts the product inside someone else's screen and gives it to clinicians free, the other sells data to pharma and feeds the data with test volume

OpenEvidence

Clinical evidence Q&A embedded in the Epic workflow · OpenEvidence (US)

Function and position. A clinician asks a complex clinical question in natural language inside Epic and gets an answer from peer-reviewed literature, fitted to that patient's context. The selling point is not reasoning quality but placement — from Oct 29 it covers NYU Langone's seven inpatient locations and more than 400 outpatient sites, 12.4 million outpatient visits a year and more than 6,000 attending physicians (Unite.AI, 2026-10-29). The model is free to clinicians, monetized through advertising, with a $250M Series D in September putting the valuation at $15 billion (Axios, 2026-09-25).

  • Strength : being inside Epic removes the one-more-tab adoption barrier, which is where clinical AI most often dies — the Jefferson Health figures cited here on Nov 1 were 5,234 provisioned accounts against 1,802 actual users (Unite.AI, 2026-10-29).
  • Strength : free to clinicians, which downgrades the procurement decision from whether to buy to whether to switch on, bypassing the hospital IT budget queue — the main reason it reached several academic centers within a year.
  • Concern : its benchmark scores do not lead. In the blinded evaluation covered here on Oct 29, OpenEvidence scored 62.6 on the 500-item open-ended HealthBench against GPT's 88.0 and UpToDate's 61.3 — it wins on distribution, not answer quality, so if Epic's own foundation models or a rival secure the same slot, the moat reduces to brand.
  • Concern : the revenue behind a $15 billion valuation is not public, and neither the ceiling on advertising monetization in a clinical setting nor the conflict-of-interest question — pharma advertising appearing beside a prescribing decision — has been disclosed. The NYU Langone agreement's value, term and paying party are likewise undisclosed.

Tempus Data & Applications（Insights／Lens／agentic tools）

Oncology multimodal data licensing and clinical applications · Tempus AI (US, NASDAQ: TEM)

Function and position. It turns the multimodal data generated by its own genomic testing business — more than 600 petabytes, with over 50 added this quarter — into datasets and analytics pharma can license, then extends into the clinic as agentic tools for trial matching and care-gap detection. On Nov 2 the segment reported $108.1M for the quarter, up 33%, with Insights up 29%, roughly $500M in year-to-date licensing bookings, and a BMS oncology data and model collaboration capped at $192.5M over five years (Investing.com, 2026-11-02).

  • Strength : it makes its own raw material. The data comes from its Diagnostics segment — $303.5M this quarter, up 20%, with oncology volume up 32% — so it neither buys data from hospitals nor lives and dies by a single system's contract, which is the hardest part for a software-only rival to copy (Investing.com, 2026-11-02).
  • Strength : the buyer is pharma rather than a hospital, with a tier better ability and speed to pay; this segment pulled the blended non-GAAP gross margin from 64% to 66% and directly produced the first positive adjusted EBITDA quarter at $25.6M.
  • Concern : the segment is only 26% of revenue, with the other 74% in a thinner-margin testing business exposed to reimbursement pricing; therapy-selection ASP stands at $1,880 against a company target above $2,600, and closing that gap depends on payer negotiation rather than technology.
  • Concern : the roughly $500M is year-to-date licensing bookings rather than recognized revenue, and recognition tracks pharma project timing; the BMS $192.5M is a five-year ceiling. For the agentic tools and foundation models, no customer count, usage figure or clinical outcome data has been published.

The two models cross at who pays. OpenEvidence drives marginal cost to near zero, gives it to clinicians free and charges a third party for advertising, so its risk is the monetization ceiling and conflicts of interest; Tempus makes pharma pay and feeds the data with its own test volume, so its risk is reimbursement pricing across that 74%. Worth noting that neither leads with having the better model — this round of clinical AI competition has already moved from the model layer to the distribution layer and the question of who owns the data.

Today's competitive structure is a fork, not a curve. General AI capital is contracting — OpenAI cut a third, the Nasdaq 100 shed $1.2 trillion — while the half of medical AI that can show unit economics is being repriced upward (HGP's 5.0x, Tempus's first positive EBITDA, Hinge Health's 87% gross margin), and the half that cannot is being taken in by strategics on channel terms (Stryker's option, Medtronic's lead, Deutsche Telekom's stake). Three roads: cash flow goes to the public market, channel value sells to the device majors, and a story alone waits for the next round.

為什麼重要Why it matters

醫療 AI 的驗證週期以年計：前瞻性試驗、IRB、多中心外部驗證、FDA 審查。若模型世代更替速率真的翻倍，那麼「驗證完成的那個模型」與「正在被使用的那個模型」會永久脫鉤——這不是實作問題，是制度的時間常數問題。本週所有關於責任、給付與認證的文件，都隱含假設模型是可以被凍結來檢查的。 Validation in medicine runs on a scale of years: prospective trials, IRB review, multi-site external validation, FDA clearance. If the generational turnover of models has genuinely doubled, the model that finished validation and the model actually in use come apart permanently — which is not an implementation problem but a mismatch of institutional time constants. Every document this week on liability, payment and certification implicitly assumes a model can be frozen long enough to be inspected.

為什麼重要Why it matters

這份稽核與本週的 Stony Brook 出院摘要研究方向完全相反，而兩者都對：LLM 在「重寫已知資訊」上勝過疲憊的住院醫師，在「回想精確數值」上則系統性失準，而新藥愈新錯得愈兇——這正是訓練資料時間分布的直接後果。醫院的部署決策因此不該問「模型夠好了嗎」，而要問「這個任務是重寫還是回想」。 The audit points the opposite way from this week's Stony Brook discharge-summary study, and both are right: an LLM beats a tired resident at rewriting information it has been given, and misses systematically when it has to recall a precise number — the newer the drug, the worse, which is a direct consequence of how training data is distributed in time. A hospital's deployment question is therefore not whether the model is good enough but whether the task is rewriting or recall.

Google 發表健康 AI 多方法評估框架：三種方法疊起來，門檻依臨床風險而非統一切點 Google publishes a multi-method framework for evaluating health AI: three methods stacked, with thresholds set by clinical risk rather than a universal cutoff

發生什麼What

11 月 4 日 Google for Health 的臨床評估主持人 Sarah Montgomery-Taylor 發表通用型生成式 AI 的健康評估框架（白皮書在 arXiv:2611.05127，與 Elizabeth McGlynn、Dana Gelb Safran 合作）。框架疊三種方法：封閉式 benchmark（如選擇題）只當基線、AI autorater 依書面 rubric 評開放式回答、專家人審含 red-teaming 處理複雜個案與安全邊界。流程上要求先畫出「Critical User Journey」——誰用、要達成什麼、經過哪些步驟，包含可能的誤用——再挑測試案例；每個應用在準確性、安全性（同時看傷害嚴重度與發生機率）、實用性三個面向評估；通關門檻依該應用的臨床風險逐關設定，不用統一切點；上線前要做 shadow trial 與臨床試點，上線後持續監測。作者指出兩位醫師在評分任務上的歧異可達 30–40% 以上，因此必須控制評分者變異。 On November 4 Sarah Montgomery-Taylor, clinical evaluation lead at Google for Health, published a framework for evaluating general-purpose generative AI in health (whitepaper at arXiv:2611.05127, with Elizabeth McGlynn and Dana Gelb Safran). It stacks three methods: closed-question benchmarks such as multiple choice serve only as a baseline, AI autoraters score open-ended answers against written rubrics, and expert human review including red-teaming handles complex cases and safety boundaries. Procedurally it requires mapping a "Critical User Journey" first — who uses the system, what they are trying to achieve, which steps they pass through, and how it is likely to be misused — before test cases are chosen; each application is assessed on accuracy, safety (weighing both severity and likelihood of harm) and usefulness; passing thresholds are set gate by gate according to that application's clinical risk rather than a universal cutoff; and evaluation runs before release through shadow trials and clinical pilots and continues as post-deployment monitoring. The authors note two physicians can diverge by 30–40% or more on a rating task, so rater variability has to be controlled for.

為什麼重要Why it matters

本週所有其他文件都在報告量測結果，只有這一份在問「該怎麼量」。它正面承接 METR 的時間常數問題：把評估拆成持續性的 gate 而非一次性認證，理論上可以跟著模型更替跑。它也正面承接 ONC 的缺口：聯邦刪掉揭露要求之後，醫院需要的正是一套可以寫進採購合約的評估方法。但它出自模型供應商，不是監管機關，也不是第三方——誰來稽核這套稽核方法，本週仍然沒有答案。 Every other document this week reports a measurement; only this one asks how to measure. It takes on METR's time-constant problem head-on by breaking evaluation into continuing gates rather than a one-time certification, which in principle can run at the pace of model turnover. It also fills the gap ONC left: once the federal disclosure requirement is deleted, what a hospital needs is exactly an evaluation method it can write into a procurement contract. But it comes from a model vendor, not a regulator and not a third party — who audits the audit method is a question this week still leaves unanswered.

02 — Product Analysis

本週兩個最值得拆的新東西，剛好站在光譜兩端：一個把分發推到執照之前，一個把資料挖得比當初開單的理由更深。 The week's two most instructive new things sit at opposite ends of the spectrum: one pushes distribution back to before licensure, the other mines data deeper than the reason it was ordered for.

OpenEvidence Learn

功能與定位。以 Penn Medicine BioBank 中近 52,000 名病人的逾 400,000 張 CT 影像訓練（該 biobank 累積逾 350,000 人、二十年資料），在背景執行，從已經做完的 CT 裡找出掃描原本不是為此而開的疾病徵象與未來風險，包括 CT 通常不會診斷的心房顫動與慢性腎病。資深作者為賓大放射科副教授、研究資訊副主任 Walter Witschey，共同作者含放射科教授 Charles Kahn，Becker's 報導。 Function and position. 04 — Taiwan Angle

本週美國在刪揭露義務，而台灣的揭露義務已經在半年前上路了——這個時間差是台灣少見的順風。 The US spent this week deleting a disclosure duty that Taiwan put into force six months ago — a rare case where the timing runs in Taiwan's favor.

(1) 台灣的揭露義務比美國早半年，而且方向相反。衛福部 2026 年 5 月 29 日以衛部醫字第 1151663164 號函公告「醫療機構應用生成式人工智慧指引」，明定若生成式 AI 工具具錄音功能，機構應事前告知病人；若 AI 工具直接與民眾互動，機構必須主動揭露對方正在與 AI 互動。對照本週 11 月 3 日 ONC 的 HTI-5 定案刪掉預測型 AI 的來源屬性揭露，兩邊走的是反方向。對打美國市場的台灣廠商而言，這意味著以台灣指引為基線開發反而不會白做——揭露功能在美國從「認證必要項」降級為「差異化選項」，但 AMA 同週把注意義務推給醫院之後，醫院採購時仍會要它。 (1) Taiwan's disclosure duty predates the US one by six months and points the opposite way. On May 29, 2026, the Ministry of Health and Welfare issued its Guidance on the Use of Generative AI in Medical Institutions (ref. 衛部醫字第1151663164號), requiring an institution to notify patients in advance where a generative AI tool has recording functionality, and to disclose proactively, where an AI tool interacts directly with members of the public, that the person is interacting with AI. Set against ONC's HTI-5, finalized November 3, which deletes source-attribute disclosure for predictive AI, the two jurisdictions are moving in opposite directions. For Taiwanese vendors selling into the US this means building to the Taiwanese guidance as a baseline is not wasted work: disclosure has been demoted there from a certification requirement to a differentiator, but after the AMA pushed the duty of care onto hospitals the same week, hospitals will still ask for it at procurement.

(2) 台灣的落地形態是語言與流程，不是模型——而這一週剛好有個乾淨的樣本。11 月 6 日 大甲李綜合醫院在健康台灣深耕計畫下導入三套 AI：大廳導引機器人、病房健教機器人，以及由易晨智能（執行長許永昌）開發的生成式 AI 語音交班紀錄系統。關鍵不在模型而在三件事：支援國語、台語、英語，語音輸入可國台語混講並處理客語，正在為移工加入印尼語與越南語；內建醫院專屬術語資料庫，由護理人員提供常用詞校準，開發約三個月；以及「獨立作業」——系統不需連上電子病歷即可運行。院方與廠方自述的數字是交班紀錄從 5 分鐘降到 1 分鐘、交班至少省 20 分鐘、新病人健教從 26 分鐘降到 20 分鐘、護理人員求援率下降 80.7%。這些是未經第三方稽核的自述值，但形態本身說明了台灣的比較優勢在哪：多語言、低整合門檻、流程貼合——不是去跟 8,954 億美元的承諾比模型。 (2) What lands in Taiwan is language and workflow, not models — and this week offered a clean sample. On November 6 Dajia Lee General Hospital deployed three AI systems under the Healthy Taiwan deep-cultivation program: a lobby guidance robot, a ward health-education robot, and a generative AI voice handover-record system built by Yi Chen Intelligence (CEO Hsu Yung-chang). What matters is not the model but three things: support for Mandarin, Taiwanese and English, with voice input able to mix Mandarin and Taiwanese and handle Hakka, and Indonesian and Vietnamese being added for migrant workers; a hospital-specific terminology database calibrated against common terms supplied by nurses, built in about three months; and standalone operation — the system runs without connecting to the electronic medical record. The hospital's and vendor's own figures are a handover record falling from 5 minutes to 1, at least 20 minutes saved on handover, health education for a new patient falling from 26 minutes to 20, and an 80.7% drop in the rate at which nursing staff request support. These are self-reported and not independently audited, but the shape itself shows where Taiwan's comparative advantage lies: many languages, a low integration threshold, a tight fit to workflow — not competing on models against $895.4B of commitments.

(3) 互通時程與本週的驗證缺口撞在一起。衛福部長石崇良在 6 月 27 日高醫大論壇表示，台灣醫學中心預計 2026 年底完成病歷互通，區域醫院 2027 年、地區醫院 2028 年跟上；行政院政務委員陳時中同場說明健康台灣深耕計畫的五年 489 億元預算，用於支持醫院發展精準醫療與遠距醫療，聯合報報導。把這條時程疊到本週的訊號上，問題很清楚：病歷互通完成那一刻，台灣醫院會同時獲得大量可供 AI 讀取的縱貫資料——這正是 Percival 那種機會性篩檢模型的原料，也正是本週 Google 評估框架所說「上線後持續監測」最需要的基礎設施。但台灣目前沒有與 HTI-5 對應的認證基準、也沒有 AMA 那樣把注意義務分配給開發者與部署者的框架；衛福部 2024 年 10 月攜 16 家醫院啟動的三大 AI 中心處理的正是落地、取證與給付三個痛點，而本週美國的經驗顯示第三個（給付）會是最慢的——Cassidy 的 PATIENT AI Act 只是討論稿，意見到 12 月 1 日才截止。 (3) The interoperability timetable collides with this week's validation gap. At a Kaohsiung Medical University forum on June 27, Minister of Health and Welfare Shih Chung-liang said Taiwan's medical centers are expected to achieve interoperable patient records by the end of 2026, with regional hospitals following in 2027 and district hospitals in 2028; at the same event Minister without Portfolio Chen Shih-chung set out the Healthy Taiwan deep-cultivation program's five-year NT$48.9 billion budget for hospital work on precision medicine and telehealth, as reported by United Daily News. Lay that timetable over this week's signals and the problem is clear: the moment interoperability is complete, Taiwanese hospitals acquire a large body of longitudinal data readable by AI — exactly the raw material an opportunistic screening model like Percival needs, and exactly the infrastructure that the "continuing post-deployment monitoring" in Google's framework most depends on. But Taiwan has no certification criteria corresponding to HTI-5 and no framework like the AMA's allocating the duty of care between developer and deployer. The three AI centers the Ministry launched with 16 hospitals in October 2024 address precisely the three pain points of deployment, clearance and reimbursement — and the US experience this week suggests the third will be the slowest: Cassidy's PATIENT AI Act is still only a discussion draft, with comments open until December 1.

  4. Conversational diagnostic AI in ambulatory primary care: a prospective feasibility study — The Lancet (2026-10-08)

But it is single-center, uncontrolled, text-only and skewed young, the authors explicitly disclaim quantitative efficacy claims, and one interaction was rated somewhat harmful by a provider because lymphoma appeared on the list of possible diagnoses and alarmed the patient.

付得更多，揭露得更少——同一個月裡，美國把醫療 AI 的名字、價錢與揭露義務推往三個不同方向 Paying more, disclosing less — in one month Washington pushed the name, the price and the disclosure duty of medical AI in three different directions

2026 年 11 月 18 日（三）· 涵蓋 10/28–11/18 · 台北時間 Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · Covering Oct 28 – Nov 18 · Taipei time

今天這一輪法規新聞可以用三個動作讀完。第一個是改名：依 2026 年 9 月 29 日的行政命令 14434「Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence」，FDA 從 11 月 16 日起在新發出的對外文件裡把「AI」寫成「SI」，同一頁明寫這不對送件者「create any new requirement」、既有指引「remain in effect as written」。第二個是喊方向：CMS 在 11 月 13 日發布的 《A Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap》，要用國家標準取代「a confusing patchwork of state rules」，並點明某些 SI 醫材的核准可能觸發對州與地方要求的明示優先排除——但這份文件自己說不強制任何人採用，也沒有一個期限。第三個才是真的動到錢與義務：11 月 6 日與 9 日公告的 CY2027 兩份最終規則給軟體開了獨立付費的身分碼 O1，最高一碼 2,250.50 美元；而 10 月 28 日公告、12 月 28 日生效的 HTI-5 把預測型決策支援工具的訓練資料與各族群表現揭露義務整個刪掉。方向相反的兩隻手，出自同一個部會。 This week's regulatory news reads as three moves. The first is a rename: under Executive Order 14434, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," signed September 29, 2026, the FDA began on November 16 writing "SI" for "AI" in newly issued public-facing documents — while the same page states the order does not "create any new requirement" for submitters and that existing guidance "remains in effect as written." The second is a direction: CMS published on November 13 A Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap, calling for national standards in place of "a confusing patchwork of state rules" and noting that some SI-enabled devices may travel pathways triggering express preemption of state and local requirements — yet the document says it mandates adoption by no one, and sets no deadline. The third actually moves money and duties: the two CY2027 final rules published November 6 and 9 give software its own separately paid status indicator, O1, topping out at $2,250.50 a code; while HTI-5, published October 28 and effective December 28, deletes the training-data and subgroup-performance disclosures required of predictive decision support tools. Two hands moving in opposite directions, from the same department.

用語變更Terminology FDAEO 1443411/16

FDA 從 11/16 起把新發文件裡的「AI」改寫成「Super Intelligence」，同一頁明寫不新增任何義務 From Nov 16 the FDA writes "Super Intelligence" for "AI" in new documents — and says on the same page that nothing new is required

發生什麼What

依 2026 年 9 月 29 日簽署的行政命令 14434，各機關須在官方文件與溝通中以「Super Intelligence」與「SI」取代「artificial intelligence」與「AI」，範圍是「to the maximum extent permitted by law」。FDA 的專頁說明自 11 月 16 日起在新發出的對外文件採用新詞，過渡期間兩種詞彙都會看到；命令第 3(a) 節以 15 U.S.C. 9401(3) 對 AI 的法定定義來定義新詞，也就是定義本身沒變。 Executive Order 14434, signed September 29, 2026, directs agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "artificial intelligence" and "AI" in official documents and communications, "to the maximum extent permitted by law." The FDA's own page states the new terms appear in newly issued public-facing documents from November 16, with both terms visible during the transition; Section 3(a) defines them by reference to the statutory definition of AI at 15 U.S.C. 9401(3) — the definition itself is unchanged.

為什麼重要Why it matters

對任何要讀美國醫材法規的人，這是檢索問題而不是合規問題：11/16 之後發出的 FDA 文件要用「SI」才找得到，但法條、現行法規、聯邦公報與既有指引仍寫「AI」，FDA 明說引用這些時照原文寫。同一套規則從這天開始有兩個名字，而兩個名字都得查。 For anyone reading US device regulation this is a retrieval problem, not a compliance one: FDA documents issued after November 16 must be searched under "SI," while statutes, current regulations, the Federal Register and existing guidance still say "AI," and the FDA says it will quote those as written. From this date one body of rules carries two names, and both have to be searched.

要打折的地方Discount this

這一則實質上什麼都沒改。FDA 自己寫明命令「does not create any new requirement for sponsors, applicants, manufacturers, or others who submit information to FDA」，包含品名與標示；既有指引、法規與聯邦公報公告「remain in effect as written」，用語只在各文件下次實質修訂時跟著改。把改名讀成監管鬆綁或加嚴，兩種都讀錯了。 Substantively, nothing changed. The FDA writes that the order "does not create any new requirement for sponsors, applicants, manufacturers, or others who submit information to FDA," including names and labeling; existing guidance, regulations and Federal Register notices "remain in effect as written," with terminology conformed only at each document's next substantive revision. Reading the rename as either loosening or tightening would be wrong both ways.

政策路線圖Policy roadmap CMSHHS11/13

CMS 的《Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap》要用國家標準取代州規則、並替「SI 主導的照護」付錢——但不強制、無期限、舉證全是 benchmark CMS's Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap seeks national standards over state rules and payment for SI-led care — mandating nothing, with no deadline, on benchmark evidence

發生什麼What

CMS 於 2026 年 11 月 13 日發布《A Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap》，分四大支柱：Earn Public Trust、Build the Data Foundation、Establish Clear Rules of the Road、Pay for What Works。付費面提出以成果為基礎的慢病管理與預防保健支付、SI 主導的 accountable care 與總額照護架構（建立在 ACCESS、AHEAD、LEAD 模型上）、Medicare 年度健康檢查的部分環節可由 SI 執行，以及與各州合作試辦替代支付模式。法規面要求以國家標準取代「a confusing patchwork of state rules」，並點明某些 SI 醫材可走「可能觸發對州或地方要求之明示優先排除」的途徑，另提 competency-based 的上市前途徑與以 PCCP 為核心的上市後監測。放行標準是「better than today」。 CMS published on November 13, 2026 A Clinical Super Intelligence Roadmap, built on four pillars: Earn Public Trust, Build the Data Foundation, Establish Clear Rules of the Road, and Pay for What Works. On payment it proposes outcome-based support for chronic disease management and preventive care, SI-led accountable care and total-cost-of-care structures building on the ACCESS, AHEAD and LEAD models, delivery of parts of the Medicare annual wellness visit by SI, and state partnerships to test alternative payment models. On regulation it calls for national standards in place of "a confusing patchwork of state rules," notes that some SI-enabled devices may pursue authorization through actions that "may trigger express preemption of state or local requirements," and floats a competency-based premarket approach with post-market surveillance anchored on predetermined change control plans. The release bar is "better than today."

為什麼重要Why it matters

這是美國第一份把「付錢給 AI 主導的照護」寫成政策方向的聯邦文件，而且它把支付與優先排除綁在同一份路線圖裡：一邊談要替 SI 主導的 ACO 付錢，一邊談用聯邦途徑排除各州自訂要求。對醫材廠商而言，這決定的是未來要過幾套關——一套還是五十套。路線圖自己列的風險清單倒是誠實：幻覺、不同族群表現不一致、隱私與資安漏洞、商業利益衝突。 This is the first federal document to make "paying for AI-led care" a stated policy direction, and it binds payment and preemption into one text: on one page, money for SI-led ACOs; on another, federal pathways that displace state-specific requirements. For device makers it decides how many gates they will face — one or fifty. The roadmap's own risk list is, to its credit, candid: hallucinations, inconsistent performance across patient populations, privacy and security vulnerabilities, and commercial conflicts of interest.

要打折的地方Discount this

這份文件沒有法律效力。分析它的律師寫得很直接：aspirational、non-binding、沒有任何採用義務，而且它自己有一節叫「What will not be done」，明寫不強制採用、不設新機關、不要求完美，也沒有任何生效日。更該打折的是舉證：路線圖引的是 o3 在複雜病例把正確診斷排第一的 60% 對內科醫師 15.9%、Microsoft MAI-DxO 的 85.5% 對醫師「約 20%」、Cedars-Sinai 的最佳治療建議 77.1% 對 67.1%——全是 benchmark 與病例小品，不是臨床結果；公眾接受度那一段引的是 2022 年 12 月的調查（60% 美國成年人不放心），在 2026 年底的文件裡已經四年。AMA 執行長 John Whyte 今年 9 月針對同一類主張的反駁正是這一點：許多研究是模擬而非盲法試驗，他說這些工具「have to be utilized in the context of a care plan that's governed by a physician」。 The document carries no legal force. The lawyers reading it put it plainly: aspirational, non-binding, no duty to adopt — and it contains a section titled "What will not be done," stating it mandates no adoption, creates no new agency, demands no perfection, and setting no effective date. The evidence deserves the sharper discount: the roadmap cites o3 ranking the final diagnosis first in 60% of complex cases against 15.9% for internal medicine physicians, Microsoft's MAI-DxO at 85.5% against "roughly 20%," and Cedars-Sinai's optimal treatment recommendations at 77.1% against 67.1% — all benchmark and vignette work, not clinical outcomes; and public comfort is sourced to a December 2022 survey (60% of US adults uncomfortable), four years old in a late-2026 document. AMA chief executive John Whyte's September rebuttal to the same class of claim went to exactly this: many of the studies are simulations rather than blinded trials, and these tools, he said, "have to be utilized in the context of a care plan that's governed by a physician."
